Posture ≠ “buy another CSPM logo” (owners, exceptions, change path)

Cloud security posture, done honestly, is not a purchase of another dashboard that ranks findings. A scanner without owners, exception paths, and a change route is noise with a license fee. Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “improve cloud security posture” as a vibe while the bottleneck is who can approve a fix, who owns a noisy finding, and what happens when production cannot change this sprint.

Typical surfaces a Zion posture conversation can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • Named cloud surface — one account, one landing-zone slice, or one tightly bounded workload family. Not “every subscription in the org chart forever.”
  • Owners for findings — who triages critical vs noisy, who can mark accepted risk, and who signs that a finding is out of scope. Without owners, CSPM becomes a shared inbox nobody clears.
  • Exception path — clear escalation when a control cannot apply yet (legacy workload, customer-mandated config, temporary break-glass). Exceptions without expiry and owners are shadow policy.
  • Change path — how a fix moves from finding → ticket or runbook → review → production. Posture without change is a report.
  • Shared-responsibility clarity — which controls sit with the cloud provider, which with your platform team, which with app owners. Ambiguity here is a Consulte signal, not a Starter line item.

What it is not: a promise of inventable vuln counts, a compliance badge you can paste on a slide, or a CSPM bake-off ranked by logos. If a vendor leads with invented scores or “certified posture” theater, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Zion’s lane is operational clarity first; see cloud security posture.

Consulte-leaning when multi-account / shared responsibility is unclear

Consulte via /contact/ is the default first move for most mid-market posture problems — not because you are “enterprise theater,” but because the real blockers are ownership and shared-responsibility design, not missing another CSPM SKU.

Consulte first when:

  • Multi-account sprawl — org units, landing zones, or legacy accounts lack a single owner who can approve hardening changes without a weekly steering fight across security, platform, and finance.
  • Shared responsibility is fuzzy — security writes policy; platform owns IaC; app teams own runtime configs; nobody can say who closes which finding class. Labels are political, not missing.
  • Exception theater — accepted risk is informal Slack lore, never expired, never reviewed. A Starter SKU cannot invent an exception board.
  • Tooling already exists; backlog still grows — you already have scanners or CSPM-like signals and the pain is triage ownership and change velocity, not “buy another logo.”
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning more than one account family, or evidence requirements before any helper touches production identity or network paths.

Consulte is not “the expensive version of Discovery.” It is the path when you need stakeholder design, control language, and a SOW shape before money hits a hardening pilot. Discovery can still be a cheap pre-read before that call; Starter can still be the first implementation after Consulte scopes it. Do not force a multi-account shared-responsibility problem into ZG06 just because the price is published.
