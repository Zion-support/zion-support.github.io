Lakehouse pipeline scope (ingest → quality → serve — no fake “unified analytics” hype)

AI data lakehouse pipelines, done honestly, are not a promise of unified analytics across every source tomorrow. They are a scoped path from ingest through quality checks to a serve surface — with humans still owning exceptions, access, and production risk.

Typical surfaces a Zion pilot can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • Ingest for one domain — a named source-to-landing path with an owner who can approve schema change and backfill decisions.
  • Quality gates — checks that fail loudly on a defined contract so bad data does not silently poison downstream AI or BI consumers.
  • Serve surface — a table, view, or feature-ready set that a named consumer can actually use — not a lake folder nobody trusts.
  • Ownership and access — who can write, who can read, who retires a pipeline — written down for the scoped domain.
  • Exception paths — clear escalation when automation should stop (PII surprises, regulated sources, a warehouse team veto, a source owner who will not grant extract rights).

What it is not: a promise to “process X TB cheaper,” a cost-per-query savings chart invented for a pitch deck, a tool-logo wall, or a full lakehouse transformation hidden inside a single SKU. If a vendor leads with invented volume math or a winner-stack slide, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Zion’s lane is operational clarity first; see AI data lakehouse pipelines.

Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “AI-ready lakehouse” as a vibe while the bottleneck is ownership: who owns the source, who can change the quality contract, who pays when a shared landing zone blurs domain boundaries. Pipelines without those answers become another job nobody monitors.

So the commercial question is not “should we build lakehouse pipelines?” It is “are sources and governance clear enough to Pay for Starter ZG06 — or do we still need Consulte / Discovery because the architecture map is politically multi-owned?”

Consulte via /contact/ is the default first move for most lakehouse pipeline conversations — not because every buyer is “enterprise,” but because unclear sources and governance make package SKUs the wrong unit.

Consulte first when:

  • Sources are multi-owned — CRM, ERP, product events, and partner feeds all claim relevance; nobody owns extract rights without a fight.

  • AI consumers are undefined — “AI-ready” has no named model, feature store, or analytics owner; the serve surface would be speculative.
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning more than one domain, or regulated evidence requirements before any helper touches production extracts.
