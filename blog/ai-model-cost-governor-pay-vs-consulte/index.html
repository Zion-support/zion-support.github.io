Model cost ≠ cloud VM cost (tokens, retries, RAG fan-out — no fake % savings)

An AI model cost governor, done honestly, is not a cloud bill widget with a new label. Cloud VM cost is hours and idle nodes. Model cost is tokens, retries, context windows, and retrieval fan-out — often on a shared key finance cannot attribute.

Typical surfaces a Zion pilot can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • One named model path — a paragraph naming the app, the model endpoint, and the call pattern (chat, batch, RAG, eval). Not “all GenAI, all keys, forever.”
  • Token and retry visibility — prompt, completion, and retry counts on that path so owners can see what actually moved the bill.
  • Budget cap or hard stop — a named threshold with a named owner who can raise, pause, or kill the path. A governor without a stop condition is a dashboard.
  • RAG and fan-out hygiene — retrieval depth, chunk count, and retry policy on the scoped path, because fan-out multiplies tokens on one user turn.
  • Exception paths — clear escalation when the governor should stop (exec demo, incident war-room, a regulated workload, a product launch that finance pre-approved).

What it is not: a promise to “cut token spend by X%,” a ranking of model vendors, or a FinOps transformation hidden inside a single SKU. If a vendor leads with invented savings percentages or a winner-model slide, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Zion’s lane is operational clarity first; see AI model cost governor.

Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “govern AI model costs” as a vibe while the bottleneck is ownership: who owns the key, who can change a retry cap, who pays when three apps share one provider account. Guardranls without those answers become another chart finance ignores.

Consulte first when:
