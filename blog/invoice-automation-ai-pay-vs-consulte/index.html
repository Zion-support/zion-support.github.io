What a scoped invoice pilot includes (capture → match → exception — no fake straight-through %)

Invoice automation AI, done honestly, is not a black box that “processes everything.” It is a scoped pipeline from capture through matching to exception handling — with humans still owning judgment calls, tax nuance, and approval exceptions.

Typical surfaces a Zion pilot can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • Capture — ingest invoices from agreed channels (email, portal, upload) into a structured intake path your team owns.
  • Extract and validate — pull key fields and flag missing or inconsistent data for human review — not silent invent.
  • Match assist — support PO / receipt / invoice matching where rules and master data are stable enough to trust.
  • Exception routing — send unclear or high-risk items to a named queue with context, not a dead-end folder.
  • Handoff into ERP/AP — write or stage into the system of record when access and field mapping are agreed — with an acceptance owner.

What it is not: a promise of “X% straight-through processing,” unsupervised payment release, or a multi-entity tax transformation hidden inside a single SKU. If a vendor leads with invented STP math and logos, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Zion’s lane is operational clarity first; see the service page for invoice automation AI.

Mid-market AP stalls when leadership buys “AI invoices” as a vibe while the actual bottleneck is ownership: who owns the vendor master, who can change match rules, who approves exceptions when the controller is out. Automation without those answers becomes another inbox the AP desk ignores under close.

So the commercial question is not “should we automate invoices?” It is “is the first improvement scoped enough to Pay for Starter ZG06, or do we still need Consulte / Discovery because the process map and ERP access are politically multi-owned?”

Consulte for multi-entity, tax edge cases, or unclear ERP access

Consulte via /contact/ is the right first move when package SKUs are the wrong unit — not because you are “enterprise theater,” but because AP’s real blockers are political and control-shaped.

Consulte first when:

  • Multi-entity maze — several legal entities, charts, or approval chains with no single process owner for invoice rules.
  • Tax and compliance edge cases — withholding, cross-border, or industry-specific invoice rules that must be designed before any helper touches production data.
  • Unclear ERP access — nobody can grant staging rights, field mapping is contested, or a SI/MSP owns the ERP console and will block a $2,500 pilot without RACI.
  • Vendor master politics — cleanup and ownership of master data are unresolved; match assist would amplify garbage-in.
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning multiple entities or regulated evidence requirements before automation touches invoice payloads.

Consulte is not “the expensive version of Discovery.” It is the path when you need stakeholder design, control language, and a SOW shape before money hits a pilot.
