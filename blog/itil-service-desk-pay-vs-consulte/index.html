ITIL desk ≠ ticket chatbot (incident/problem/change ownership)

An ITIL service desk, done honestly, is not a chatbot that “handles everything.” It is a working agreement about three processes that keep colliding on mid-market desks: incident (restore service), problem (find the cause), and change (alter production with a named risk owner). If those three have no owners, a bot only accelerates the wrong work.

Typical surfaces a Zion process engagement can touch (named, with a definition of done):

  • Incident ownership — who is major-incident commander, who pages after hours, who can declare “service restored,” and what is not an incident.
  • Problem ownership — who may open a problem record, who owns the known-error note, and who can say “stop firefighting, this is systemic.”
  • Change ownership — who proposes, who assesses risk, who sits on the change board, who can emergency-change, and who owns a failed change.
  • RACI that a human can use next week — responsible / accountable / consulted / informed for the three processes on one desk, not a 40-row matrix. Adjacent: managed IT services.
  • Exception paths — what happens when the board cannot convene, a vendor change lands unannounced, or security vetoes a restore.

What it is not: a ticket chatbot, a “deflect X% of tickets” promise, an ITIL Foundation class sold as implementation, or a multi-queue transformation hidden inside a single SKU. If a vendor leads with certification badges and staffing cuts, treat that as marketing — not a process SOW. See IT ITIL service desk.

Mid-market desks stall when leadership buys “ITIL helpdesk” as a vibe while the bottleneck is political: who owns major incident, who can freeze a change window, who pays when a problem record never gets a root cause. Tooling without those answers becomes another ignored queue.

So the commercial question is not “should we implement ITIL?” It is “is process broken enough that Consulte must come first, or is one queue owned well enough that Pay ZG06 can land?” If you came here looking for bots and routing, that is Wave 6 — service desk automation — Pay vs Consulte — not this one.

Consulte-leaning when RACI / change board is broken

This leaf is Consulte-leaning by default. Process redesign is stakeholder design. Package SKUs are the wrong unit when nobody can name who is accountable for incident, problem, or change. Consulte via /contact/ is the honest first move — a $2,500 SKU cannot invent a working change board.

Consulte first when:

  • RACI is theater — a slide exists; live tickets still bounce between IT, security, the business, and an MSP with no accountable name. Related: co-managed IT vs MSP.
  • Change board is broken — it never meets, it always approves, it has no emergency path, or CAB and security disagree on what “standard change” means. A Starter SKU cannot referee that.
  • Incident and problem are collapsed — every repeat ticket is a new fire; nobody is allowed to open a problem or own a known error. Process design has to land before any tooling tweak.
  • Multi-queue / multi-vendor politics — regional desks, shadow catalogs, or a co-managed MSP that owns the console while internal IT owns the process narrative. Those two stories must be one RACI before Pay.
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning more than one process, or regulated evidence requirements before any helper reads ticket or change data.

Consulte is not “the expensive version of Discovery.” It is the path when you need stakeholder design, control language, and a SOW shape before money hits a pilot. Discovery can still be a cheap pre-read; Starter can still be downstream after Consulte scopes a single owned queue. Do not force a broken change board into ZG06 just because the price is published.

Pay ZG06 only when one queue + tooling owner + success metric exist

The Pay path is Starter — $2,500 (ZG06), and on this leaf it is the exception, not the headline. It implements one agreed process-or-tooling improvement with clear inputs, owners, and a definition of done. Detail on plans.

Pay ZG06 for an ITIL service desk item only when you already know:

  • One queue in scope — one paragraph naming the queue and the process it serves (P1/P2 incident on the core IT queue, or standard-change on a named catalog). Not “all of ITSM.”
  • Tooling owner identified — who owns the ITSM console, who can grant change rights, and whether a process change in that queue is allowed this quarter. If an MSP holds the keys, name that in the SOW or do not Pay yet.
  • Process already has a human owner — someone who can say yes/no on scope without a weekly CAB argument.
  • Success metric agreed — an observable operating signal on that queue (time-to-commander on major incident, problem records opened for repeats) — not an invented ticket-deflection percentage.
  • Acceptance owner named — someone on your side signs that the deliverable matches the SOW.

If any of those five is missing, you are not on Pay — you are on Discovery or Consulte. Starter is not a blank check to “explore ITIL service desk implementation.” Same rule: Pay vs Consulte.

What good looks like after a rare Pay kickoff: one live improvement on one owned queue, named owners for exceptions, and a short handoff note the desk can use next week. What bad looks like: a pilot SOW that still says “implement ITIL” inside the implementation fee. Price evaluation as Discovery; price process design as Consulte; price a scoped build as Starter.

Ready path if (and only if) the five bullets are true: review plans for Starter ZG06, or Consulte if procurement needs a human first.
