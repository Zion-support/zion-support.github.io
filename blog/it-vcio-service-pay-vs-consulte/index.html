What vCIO covers (roadmap, vendors, risk — not helpdesk tickets)

IT vCIO service, done honestly, is not “buy another tool and hope the stack coheres.” It is fractional CIO judgment: roadmap priorities, vendor choices, and risk tradeoffs — with humans still owning board-level decisions and production exceptions.

Typical surfaces a Zion vCIO engagement can touch (advisory, with clear owners):

  • Roadmap sequencing — what to fund this quarter vs next, written so ops and finance stop debating in Slack.
  • Vendor and stack choices — which tools stay, which consolidate, which wait — without a fake bake-off ranking sold as proof.
  • Risk and control language — what must be true before a new platform, cloud move, or automation pilot touches production.
  • Leadership coverage — a named advisory cadence for founders/COOs who do not yet need (or cannot hire) a full-time CIO.
  • Exception paths — clear escalation when advice should stop and ownership must return to the client (regulated decisions, board veto, a partner contract the advisor cannot sign).

What it is not: helpdesk ticket queues, an invented hours/month retainer beyond what plans already publishes, an MSP ROI percentage, or “replace your IT team with a dashboard.” If a vendor leads with fake CIO logos or certified savings slides, treat that as marketing — not an advisory SOW.
