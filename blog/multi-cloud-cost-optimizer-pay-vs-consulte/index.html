What multi-cloud optimization actually covers (tagging, idle, commitment — not fake % savings)

A multi-cloud cost optimizer, done honestly, is not a console that “finds waste across every vendor.” It is a scoped set of improvements to how accounts are tagged, how idle capacity is named and retired, and how commitment decisions get an owner — with humans still owning exceptions and production risk.

Typical surfaces a Zion pilot can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • Tagging and account hygiene — a named tag or label contract on one cloud pair so spend can be attributed without a weekly spreadsheet fight.
  • Idle and unowned surfaces — name accounts, subscriptions, or projects that look abandoned so owners can confirm retire or keep.
  • Commitment review assist — reserved instances, savings plans, or committed-use decisions on a defined envelope — presented as options an owner can accept or reject, not silent auto-commit.
  • Showback / chargeback hygiene — a first honest allocation view for the scoped pair when finance and platform already agree who will read it. Adjacent when the operating model is the product: FinOps consulting.
  • Exception paths — clear escalation when automation should stop (regulated workloads, burst events, shared-org vetoes, a cloud team that will not grant change rights).

What it is not: a promise to “cut multi-cloud spend by X%,” a ranking of AWS vs Azure vs GCP, a vendor bake-off, or a FinOps transformation hidden inside a single SKU. If a vendor leads with invented recovery dollars or a winner-cloud slide, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Zion’s lane is operational clarity first; see multi-cloud cost optimizer.

Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “multi-cloud FinOps” as a vibe while the bottleneck is ownership: who owns the billing org, who can change a tag, who pays when a shared account blurs chargeback. Optimization without those answers becomes another dashboard finance ignores.

So the commercial question is not “should we optimize multi-cloud spend?” It is “is the first improvement scoped enough to Pay for Starter ZG06, or do we still need Consulte / Discovery because the spend map and FinOps RACI are politically multi-owned?”

Consulte when shared billing orgs / chargeback fights / unclear FinOps RACI

Consulte via /contact/ is the right first move when package SKUs are the wrong unit — not because you are “enterprise theater,” but because the spend’s real blockers are political and control-shaped.

Consulte first when:

  • Shared billing orgs — consolidated billing, management groups, or organization units serve multiple business units; nobody owns idle retire or tag-policy decisions without a fight.
  • Chargeback fights — finance, platform, and app owners disagree on allocation keys before any idle or commitment work can stick. Labels are political, not missing.
  • Unclear FinOps RACI — cloud teams, finance, and product all claim the same waste narrative; nobody owns exceptions after hours or after a burst. Adjacent operating-model work: FinOps consulting.
  • Vendor-spanning politics — AWS, Azure, and GCP (or hybrid) teams disagree on which cost signal is the tool of record. A Starter SKU cannot pick a winner-cloud for you.
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning more than one pair, or regulated evidence requirements before any helper touches production signals.

Consulte is not “the expensive version of Discovery.” It is the path when you need stakeholder design, control language, and a SOW shape before money hits a pilot. Discovery can still be a cheap pre-read before that call; Starter can still be the first implementation after Consulte scopes it. Do not force a shared-billing political problem into ZG06 just because the price is published.
