What “the desk” must mean before you Pay

Pay is only honest when the desk is a named catalog, not a mood. Write one page that a service owner can defend tomorrow:

  • Which L1 patterns are in scope (access, password/reset, known errors, standard software requests) — and which are out.
  • Which system of record the helper reads (ITSM, identity, email, chat) and who grants that access.
  • What the helper may do alone vs what requires a human gate (especially privileged or production-adjacent actions).
  • Who accepts “done” — a named person, not “the service desk.”

If that page is half blank, or three owners would rewrite it, you are not on Pay. You are on Discovery or Consulte. The commercial mistake is buying Starter ZG06 to settle an ownership argument that still lives in Slack.

A practical filter: pick the last twenty tickets that look “easy.” If you cannot group them into three written patterns with a routing rule and an exception owner, the catalog is not ready. Mapping those twenty tickets is Discovery work, not a $2,500 implementation pretending to be a map.

Pay path — Starter ZG06 for a catalogued desk

Pay Starter ZG06 when the desk already has a catalog, an access path, and a human exception lane.

Starter — $2,500 (ZG06) is a project to implement one agreed automation or ops improvement. For the service desk that usually means one of:

  • Triage + enrich + route for a written L1 catalog, with humans on exceptions.
  • A known-error or access-request helper that drafts or files — not unsupervised privileged closes.
  • A single queue’s routing rules, with a named acceptor for the definition of done.

Pay fails when kickoff still debates who owns identity, whether the MSP closes the ticket, or whether the helper may reset or provision. That debate belongs in Discovery or Consulte, not inside a $2,500 SOW.

Pay also implies you will protect the pilot from scope creep. One catalog means one. Adding “while you’re in there” VIP desks, vendor queues, and privileged writes turns ZG06 into an unpaid Consulte engagement. Consulte path — when the desk is a maze

Consulte via /contact/ when the commercial unit is a conversation and a custom SOW — not a checkout.

Consulte fits a service desk when:

  • Multi-owner close path — the internal desk, an MSP, and identity/IAM disagree on who is allowed to close or reopen.
  • Privileged actions — password reset, group membership, production access, or vendor VPN would be in the helper’s reach without a written gate.
  • Unclear catalog — “L1” is actually five variants across sites, languages, or business units with no single owner.
  • Regulated or high-audit — evidence, retention, and who touched what must be designed before any write path.
  • Vendor maze — existing MSPs or SIs already own part of the stack; a package pilot will collide without stakeholder design.

Consulte does not mean “we never Pay.” Many desks Consulte first, then land Starter ZG06 once the catalog and gates are honest. The mistake is using Consulte as endless free discovery — or avoiding Consulte when Pay would put an unsupervised closer on privileged work.

Bring to Consulte: twenty sample tickets, the systems you think are involved, who must approve production or identity changes, and what “stop / proceed to Pay” would look like. That keeps Consulte from becoming a tour of every AI idea in the company. Coverage context: managed IT services.

Fit patterns (catalogued L1 vs tangled desk)

Patterns only — no client logos, no invented before/after metrics.

Catalogued L1. Fit for Pay/Starter when access, reset, and known-error patterns are written, routing rules can be stated, and humans stay on exceptions. The helper triages, enriches, and routes. It does not silently close privileged work.

Tangled or privileged desk. Fit for Consulte when the close path is political, when IAM and the desk disagree, or when the “easy” tickets include group changes, production access, or vendor credentials. Design the gate first. Product language for agent gates: autonomous AI agents and AI agent governance.

MSP-shared desk. Fit for Consulte (or Discovery first) when an external provider already owns SLAs, after-hours, or identity. A package pilot that ignores that contract creates two sources of truth. Managed context: managed IT and AI automation for MSPs.

Across all three: if the catalog is clear and one owner can accept done → Pay path. If ownership and controls are the product → Consulte (or Discovery first).

What good looks like after kickoff

Good after kickoff is process clarity — not a manufactured deflection chart.

  • Written catalog that still matches day 10. The L1 list did not mutate into a second queue. Change requests are named, not smuggled.
  • Access landed. ITSM / identity / chat needed for the helper are reachable; blocked access has a named owner.
  • Definition of done is operational. A live routing or draft path, with who reviews exceptions. Not “we explored AI on the desk.”
  • Exception queue exists. Humans know what the helper must not decide alone — especially privileged actions.
  • Next track is explicit. Stop, extend via Consulte/SOW, or move toward coverage under managed IT — decided with a map, not momentum.

We will not claim a deflection rate, a savings percentage, or a certified outcome. If your sponsor needs board language without fake math, start from Discovery’s written map and the operating signal you chose at kickoff — then report what actually moved.

Bad after kickoff looks familiar: access still pending after two weeks, success redefined to “demo,” exceptions closed without review, or a second queue bolted on without a change order. Stop and re-scope — Consulte or a new SOW — rather than burning the Pay path’s credibility.
