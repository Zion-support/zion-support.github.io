1. Capture the minimum dispatch-ready scope

Site & access

City/site, access window, onsite contact, security or escort constraints, loading/parking needs and remote-hands prerequisites.

Technical task

Device, rack, circuit, endpoint or software target; known symptoms; expected action; rollback boundary; required tools or spares.

Evidence

Photos, serials, logs, diagrams, ticket number, test criteria and the exact proof required before checkout.

Commercial gate

Written approval, PO or equivalent authorization before purchase, dispatch or irreversible work. Technical diagnosis does not equal purchase authorization.

4. Field-services decision checklist

  1. Can the work be remote? If yes, test remote diagnostics first and avoid an unnecessary dispatch.
  2. Is the site information complete? If no, build the site-survey brief before scheduling.
  3. Is the acceptance test written? Define what proves the work is complete before the visit starts.
  4. Are spares or licenses required? Separate sourcing from the engineer visit and validate compatibility, warranty, taxes, freight and lead time.
  5. Is there formal authorization? Do not treat a technical recommendation as authorization to purchase or dispatch.
