PAM ≠ “buy a vault logo” (owners, break-glass, review cadence)

Privileged access management, done honestly, is not a purchase of another password vault that sits beside the last one. A vault without owners, break-glass paths, and a review cadence is theater with a license fee. Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “PAM implementation” as a vibe while the bottleneck is who owns standing admin, who can approve just-in-time elevation, and what happens when production break-glass must fire at 2 a.m.

Typical surfaces a Zion PAM conversation can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • Named admin domain — one Active Directory slice, one cloud IAM tenant boundary, or one tightly bounded SaaS admin seat family. Not “every privileged account in the org chart forever.”
  • Owners for privileged accounts — who approves standing vs just-in-time admin access, who can mark a shared account as retired, and who signs that a privilege is out of scope. Without owners, privileged account management becomes a shared spreadsheet nobody clears.
  • Break-glass path — clear escalation when normal approval cannot apply yet (incident, outage, temporary contractor). Break-glass without expiry, logging, and owners is shadow privilege.
  • Review cadence — how often standing rights get re-attested, who runs the review, and what happens when an owner leaves. PAM without review is a vault full of stale keys.
  • Just-in-time admin access intent — which roles should elevate on demand versus remain standing, and who owns the approval chain. Ambiguity here is a Consulte signal, not a Starter line item.

What it is not: a promise of inventable breach stats, a cert badge you can paste on a slide, or a PAM bake-off ranked by logos. If a vendor leads with invented incident percentages or “certified PAM” theater, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Consulte-leaning when multi-domain / shared admin politics unclear

Consulte via /contact/ is the default first move for most mid-market PAM problems — not because you are “enterprise theater,” but because the real blockers are ownership and shared-admin design, not missing another vault SKU.

Consulte first when:

  • Multi-domain sprawl — on-prem directory, cloud IAM, and SaaS admin seats lack a single owner who can approve privilege changes without a weekly steering fight across security, IT, and app owners.
  • Shared admin politics are fuzzy — security writes policy; IT owns the directory; app teams own local admin; nobody can say who retires which standing right. Labels are political, not missing.
  • Break-glass theater — emergency admin is informal Slack lore, never expired, never reviewed. A Starter SKU cannot invent a break-glass board.
  • Tooling already exists; standing rights still grow — you already have a vault or PAM-like signals and the pain is triage ownership and review cadence, not “buy another logo.”
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning more than one admin domain, or evidence requirements before any helper touches production privilege paths.

Consulte is not “the expensive version of Discovery.” It is the path when you need stakeholder design, privilege language, and a SOW shape before money hits a PAM pilot.
