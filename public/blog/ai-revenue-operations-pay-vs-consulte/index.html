What a scoped RevOps pilot includes (hygiene → routing → forecast inputs — no fake pipeline %)

AI revenue operations, done honestly, is not a purchase of another dashboard that claims inventable pipeline percentages. A model without hygiene ownership, routing rules, and forecast-input discipline is noise with a license fee. Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “RevOps AI” as a vibe while the bottleneck is who owns dirty fields, who can change lead routing, and what the forecast actually consumes.

Typical surfaces a Zion RevOps pilot can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • CRM hygiene — named fields, required stages, and who cleans duplicates or stale opportunities. Without hygiene ownership, any AI for RevOps pipeline work amplifies garbage.
  • Routing — lead or opportunity assignment rules on one CRM surface, with a named owner who can change them without a weekly territory fight.
  • Forecast inputs — which stage signals, probability sources, or activity fields feed the forecast — and who can change that contract. Forecast theater without input ownership is a Consulte signal.
  • One CRM surface — a paragraph naming the CRM instance or business unit slice and what is out of scope. Not “every revenue system in the org chart forever.”
  • Definition of done — what “live” means for the pilot (hygiene rule live, routing path live, or forecast input contract written). Revenue operations automation without done criteria becomes another unread export.

What it is not: a promise of inventable win-rate lifts, a pipeline dollar guarantee, or a CRM bake-off ranked by logos. If a vendor leads with invented conversion percentages or “certified RevOps AI” theater, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Consulte when multi-CRM / territory politics unclear

Consulte via /contact/ is the right first move when the blockers are ownership and territory design, not missing another RevOps SKU.

Consulte first when:

  • Multi-CRM sprawl — legacy CRM, new CRM, and spreadsheet “systems of truth” lack a single owner who can approve routing or forecast-input changes without a weekly steering fight.
  • Territory politics are fuzzy — sales writes rules; marketing owns leads; RevOps owns stages; finance owns forecast — and nobody can say who closes which hygiene class. Labels are political, not missing.
  • Forecast theater — stage probabilities are informal lore, never reviewed, never owned. A Starter SKU cannot invent a forecast board.
  • Tooling already exists; hygiene still decays — you already have enrichment or scoring tools and the pain is ownership and change velocity, not “buy another logo.”
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning more than one CRM family, or evidence requirements before any helper touches production routing.
