Two honest commercial paths

Zion’s commercial surface for AI automation is intentionally split.

Pay means you buy a published package with known price and expected shape — most often Starter $2,500 (ZG06) for one scoped automation or ops improvement. You are saying: the problem, systems, and acceptance criteria are clear enough that implementation should start.

Consulte means you start a human-scoped conversation via /contact/. You are saying: the SKU is unclear, stakeholders disagree, controls matter before code, or the SOW will not fit a package. Consulte is not a delay tactic when used correctly. It is the path when forcing Pay would create a bad pilot.

These are not two CTAs for the same deliverable. If a page asks you to “Pay or Consulte” as if they were twins, ignore the sameness and ask: is my problem SOW-ready? If yes → Pay path. If no → Consulte (or Discovery as the cheap map). Catalog lanes under services support both paths; the path choice is commercial, not decorative.

A practical filter: write the one-page SOW you would sign tomorrow. If you can fill problem, systems, access, exception owner, and done criteria without a workshop, you are on Pay. If the page is half blank or three teams must rewrite it, you are on Consulte — or Discovery as the cheap map between them.

Pay path — Starter ZG06

Pay Starter ZG06 when problem, KPI (or operating signal), and systems are already clear.

Starter — $2,500 (ZG06) is a project to implement one agreed automation or ops improvement. It expects:

  • A one-paragraph problem statement your ops lead can defend.
  • Named systems and an access path (who grants tickets, CRM, identity, billing, or tool access).
  • An observable success signal — queue quality, exception handling, routing accuracy, spend alerts — without inventing a savings percentage.
  • A named acceptor on your side for the definition of done.

Pay fails when kickoff still debates which process to touch, who owns the data, or whether the helper may write. That debate belongs in Discovery or Consulte, not inside a $2,500 SOW pretending to be a pilot.

Pay also implies you will protect the pilot from scope creep. One automation means one. Adding “while you’re in there” desk, FinOps, and agent work turns ZG06 into an unpaid Consulte engagement. Split those into a second SOW or move to Consulte before you pretend the package still fits.

Consulte path

Consulte via /contact/ when the commercial unit is a conversation and a custom SOW — not a checkout.

Consulte fits when:

  • Unclear SKU — you do not know yet whether the work is RPA-style automation, service desk helpers, FinOps controls, managed IT coverage, or a blend under AI consulting.
  • Multi-team — IT, security, finance, and a business owner all must agree before anything touches production data.
  • Regulated or high-audit — evidence, retention, and exception queues must be designed before Pay.
  • Vendor maze — existing MSPs, SIs, or cloud partners already own part of the stack; a package pilot will collide without stakeholder design.
  • Scope larger than Starter — multiple workstreams, phased rollout, or operating-model change before automation.

Consulte does not mean “we never Pay.” Many engagements Consulte first, then land a Starter ZG06 or a larger SOW once the map is honest. The mistake is using Consulte as endless free discovery — or avoiding Consulte when Pay would be dishonest.

Bring to Consulte: the pain in one paragraph, the systems you think are involved, who must approve production changes, and what “stop / proceed to Pay” would look like. That keeps Consulte from becoming an open-ended tour of every AI idea in the company.

What good looks like after kickoff

Good after kickoff is process clarity — not a manufactured ROI chart.

  • Written scope that still matches day 10. The problem paragraph did not mutate into a second project. Change requests are named, not smuggled.
  • Access landed. Systems needed for the automation are reachable; blocked access is escalated with a named owner, not silent drift.
  • Definition of done is operational. A live workflow, governed helper, routing path, or spend alert — with who reviews exceptions. Not “we explored AI.”
  • Exception queue exists. Humans know what the automation must not decide alone.
  • Next track is explicit. Stop, extend via Consulte/SOW, or move toward ongoing coverage under managed IT — decided with a map, not momentum.

We will not claim a deflection rate, a savings percentage, or a certified outcome. If your internal sponsor needs board language without fake math, start from Discovery’s written map and the operating signal you chose at kickoff — then report what actually moved.

Bad after kickoff looks familiar: access still pending after two weeks, success redefined to “demo,” exceptions closed without review, or a second process bolted on without a change order. Stop and re-scope — Consulte or a new SOW — rather than burning the Pay path’s credibility.
