What “K8s cost optimizer” means (rightsizing, idle, namespace ownership — not magic savings %)

A Kubernetes cost optimizer, done honestly, is not a dashboard that “finds X% waste.” It is a set of scoped improvements to how workloads request resources, how idle capacity is detected, and how namespaces carry ownership — with humans still owning exceptions and production risk.

Typical surfaces a Zion pilot can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • Rightsizing assist — surface request/limit mismatches and recommend safer adjustments where owners can act, not silent auto-mutate production.
  • Idle and zombie detection — name workloads, namespaces, or environments that look abandoned so owners can confirm retire or keep.
  • Namespace ownership hygiene — tags, labels, and RACI so spend can be attributed without a weekly spreadsheet fight.
  • Schedule and scale hygiene — non-prod schedules, HPA/VPA readiness checks, or environment-level guardrails when the rule set is owned.
  • Exception paths — clear escalation when automation should stop (burst events, regulated workloads, shared-cluster vetoes).

What it is not: a promise to “cut Kubernetes spend by X%,” a multi-cloud FinOps transformation hidden inside a single SKU, or a bake-off against every cost tool on the market. If a vendor leads with invented recovery dollars and logos, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Zion’s lane is operational clarity first; see the service page for Kubernetes cost optimizer.

Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “K8s FinOps” as a vibe while the actual bottleneck is ownership: who owns the namespace, who can change requests without a change circus, who pays when shared clusters blur chargeback. Optimization without those answers becomes another dashboard finance ignores under load.

So the commercial question is not “should we optimize Kubernetes?” It is “is the first improvement scoped enough to Pay for Starter ZG06, or do we still need Consulte / Discovery because the spend map and FinOps RACI are politically multi-owned?”

Consulte when multi-cloud politics / shared clusters / unclear FinOps RACI

Consulte via /contact/ is the right first move when package SKUs are the wrong unit — not because you are “enterprise theater,” but because the spend’s real blockers are political and control-shaped.

Consulte first when:

  • Multi-cloud politics — AWS, Azure, GCP (or hybrid) teams disagree on who owns container spend signals and which tool of record wins.
  • Shared clusters without RACI — multiple business units share nodes or namespaces; nobody owns idle retire decisions without a fight.
  • Unclear FinOps RACI — platform, finance, and app owners all claim the same waste narrative; nobody owns exceptions after hours or after a burst.
  • Chargeback / showback contested — labels and cost allocation are political before any rightsizing work can stick. Adjacent framing: multi-cloud cost optimizer.
