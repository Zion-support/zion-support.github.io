AI agent risk checklist: prepare a pilot you can review

Turn an AI idea into one bounded experiment. Use this checklist to describe the job, gather evidence and decide whether the next step is ready. Keep unresolved questions visible throughout the trial.

Start with a job you can observe

Choose a small workflow with a clear input and an output someone can check.

Describe the output

For example: produce a ticket summary containing the issue, existing owner and stated deadline. Specify how missing information should appear.

Set the comparison

Record the current process and a measurable success criterion. Compare the trial with that baseline using the same kind of example.

Name the owner

Identify who reviews the output, handles exceptions and can stop the trial. Record the version and date so the result remains traceable.

Separate preparation from execution

Reading, drafting, sending and changing a record are separate actions. List the tools involved and the authorization each action requires under your organization’s rules. Test only the actions included in the agreed pilot.

Check the actual connection and permissions before relying on an integration. A published page, repository or draft output does not establish that a connected action works. Keep the tested scope explicit.

Six checks before the trial

For each area, keep the evidence reference, result, owner and next action in your approved workspace.

Data access

Evidence to collect
Named sources, permitted records and retention rules; a fictional or authorized sanitized test input.
Pause when
The source, permission or handling of the input is unclear.

Actions

Evidence to collect
Each tool action, its scope, required authorization and the person responsible for exceptions.
Pause when
The workflow requests an action outside the agreed scope.

Output quality

Evidence to collect
Expected results, observed outputs and examples with missing, invalid or conflicting information.
Pause when
The output invents a fact or fails the agreed acceptance check.

Spend

Evidence to collect
Volume and unit-cost assumptions, retry limits, an agreed budget and a budget owner.
Pause when
Usage exceeds the agreed limit or cannot be attributed.

Audit and recovery

Evidence to collect
A record of inputs, actions and decisions, plus a tested way to stop and recover.
Pause when
A material action cannot be reconstructed or safely stopped.

Ownership

Evidence to collect
A reviewer, an exception route and the recorded decision for the next step.
Pause when
No responsible person can resolve the open issue.

Worked example: a ticket summary

Fictional input: Ticket DEMO-104 reports an intermittent printer connection. Maya owns the investigation. No deadline is stated.

Expected output: preserve the issue and Maya’s ownership, identify the deadline as unknown, and suggest a question for the reviewer.

A useful result says “Deadline: not provided.” An invented date fails this check even if the summary otherwise reads well.

Record the actual output and reviewer decision. Creating a summary or email draft does not send a message or update the ticket.

Make the next decision from evidence

Continue within the tested scope

Record the observed result and the reviewer’s decision. Extend the trial only when the new scope and applicable authorization are established.

Resolve an open question

Assign an owner and next check to missing evidence. An unchecked item remains unverified; a count of completed checks does not approve the pilot.

Stop and recover

Use the agreed stop condition, preserve the relevant evidence and follow the recovery plan. Record what must change before another attempt.

Common questions

Is Discovery free?

Yes. Discovery is a free online questionnaire with eight questions, taking about five minutes. The report appears on screen. Any later paid service has its own scope and commercial agreement.

How does the Discovery report reach my team?

Email delivery can be requested to the participant and Zion Commercial. Provider acceptance is not confirmation of inbox delivery. Keep the on-screen report and use the available copy or download option.

Does completing this guide approve a production deployment?

No. This is a static planning guide with no submission form or connected actions. Your organization’s responsible people decide using the evidence, tested scope and applicable authorization.
