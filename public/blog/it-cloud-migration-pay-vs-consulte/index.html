Pilot vs program (one workload vs estate) — no fake “60% faster” claims

Scoped IT cloud migration, done honestly, is not “lift the estate and hope.” It is a defined move of one named app or workload — with humans still owning cutover risk, rollback, and production exceptions.

Typical surfaces a Zion pilot can touch (one at a time, with a definition of done):

  • One named workload — a paragraph naming the app, environment, and dependency boundary. Not “all VMs, all accounts, forever.”
  • Landing-zone fit check — whether the target account or subscription already exists and who can grant change rights this quarter.
  • Cutover and rollback ownership — who signs go/no-go, who owns rollback, who is on-call when the first business day after move hits.
  • Operating signal agreed — workload reachable on the target, owners named, handoff note usable next week — observable after kickoff, not an invented “60% faster” claim.
  • Exception paths — clear escalation when automation or policy should stop (regulated data, partner contracts, a security veto, a BU that will not grant change rights).

What it is not: a promise to “complete X% of the estate,” a downtime-minute SLA sold as proof of competence, a multi-vendor cloud bake-off, or a multi-year program RFP hidden inside a single SKU. If a vendor leads with invented migration speed percentages or a winner-cloud slide, treat that as marketing — not a pilot SOW. Zion’s lane is operational clarity first; see IT cloud migration.

Mid-market teams stall when leadership buys “migrate to cloud” as a vibe while the bottleneck is ownership: who owns the landing zone, who can approve network changes, who pays when shared accounts blur BU boundaries. Migration without those answers becomes another stalled program deck.

So the commercial question is not “should we migrate?” It is “is the first workload scoped enough to Pay for Starter ZG06, or do we still need Consulte / Discovery because architecture options and landing-zone RACI are politically multi-owned?”

Consulte for multi-account, compliance, or unclear landing-zone ownership

Consulte via /contact/ is the right first move when package SKUs are the wrong unit — not because you are “enterprise theater,” but because the real blockers are political and control-shaped.

Consulte first when:

  • Multi-account sprawl — workloads span accounts or subscriptions; nobody owns landing-zone standards without a fight.
  • Compliance gates — security, risk, and infra disagree on what evidence is required before any move can stick. Labels are political, not missing.
  • Unclear landing-zone ownership — platform, security, and app owners disagree on who signs network, IAM, and cutover before any pilot. A Starter SKU cannot invent that RACI for you.
  • Sprawling RACI — infra, security, and BU leads all claim the same migration narrative; nobody owns exceptions after hours or after a partner break.
  • Custom SOW needed — larger than Starter, spanning more than one workload, or regulated evidence requirements before any helper touches production.

Consulte is not “the expensive version of Discovery.” It is the path when you need stakeholder design, control language, and a SOW shape before money hits a pilot. Discovery can still be a cheap pre-read before that call; Starter can still be the first implementation after Consulte scopes it.
