Planning & delivery · Practical guide

Turn an AI idea into a measurable pilot

A pilot should answer a business question. Can a team finish a specific task more reliably, with less effort, or with a better result? Start with a limited workflow and make success measurable before expanding the scope.

In this guide
  1. 1. Write the decision you need to make
  2. 2. Design review, escalation, and recovery
  3. 3. Review evidence before expanding

1. Write the decision you need to make

State the problem, the people affected, and what a useful result looks like. Record the current process so that the comparison includes the work people do before and after the AI step.

  • Select one workflow and assign a business owner.
  • Measure current task volume, completion time, and rework.
  • Choose an acceptance threshold and a review date.
  • List dependencies, data sources, and the people who approve access.

2. Design review, escalation, and recovery

Agree who reviews outputs, what the system may do, and when it must stop or ask for help. Run initial evaluations with approved examples and keep a usable manual process available.

  • Define the actions allowed during the pilot.
  • Route uncertain or sensitive cases to a named reviewer.
  • Record errors and the time needed to resolve them.
  • Define how to pause the pilot and return work to the existing process.

3. Review evidence before expanding

Compare accepted outcomes against the baseline. Include operating cost, review time, integration maintenance, and exceptions. An improvement on a few easy examples does not establish readiness for a broader rollout.

  • Review results with the people who performed and accepted the work.
  • Document unresolved issues and the changes needed to address them.
  • Choose to expand, revise, or stop based on the agreed criteria.
  • For expansion, specify the next scope, owner, budget, and monitoring needs.
