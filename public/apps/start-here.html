Six short journeys, with the next step linked

Each journey moves from a question to a practical checklist. Begin with example or authorized data; keep human review in the loop.

1. Field service readiness

Can someone safely perform a specific on-site visit?

Record each address, local time window, ticket, rack or device model, required tools, site access and contact. Obtain named technicians with written availability and relevant experience.

Read the field-service evidence guide → check SLA assumptions → prepare a service brief.

No technician booking or dispatch without written client approval, applicable PO and separate Zion authorization.

2. Supplier quotes and procurement

Which offer is genuinely comparable?

Verify labor, equipment, travel, freight, taxes, import duties, overtime, cancellation and exclusions. Ask for written validity and payment terms before comparing offers.

Open the quote evidence workbench → review sourcing controls → record the decision.

Estimates are not supplier bids, purchasing approval or a final client quote.

3. IT operations and service desk

Which ticket or incident should the team handle first?

Choose a sample queue, document severity and escalation ownership, then test a response runbook with a human reviewer and a rollback path.

Explore ticket triage → review a runbook → check permissions.

Tool pages are not proof of a connected service desk or measured production SLA.

4. FinOps and value assumptions

Where should cost analysis start?

Define a measured baseline and period first. Separate cloud bills, forecast assumptions and one-time implementation costs from realized savings.

Try the FinOps estimator → compare ROI scenarios → document assumptions.

Calculator outputs are illustrative, not audited outcomes or guaranteed savings.

5. AI discovery and a reversible pilot

What is the smallest useful test?

Start with a free, online Discovery report. Identify one process owner, one baseline, an objective, authorized or synthetic test data and a stop rule.

Start free Discovery → write the pilot brief → plan a limited test.

The report appears in the browser; email-provider acceptance does not prove inbox delivery.

6. Communications workflows

Where do calls, messages and meeting actions get lost?

Map a single handoff and measure missed routing, follow-up ownership or meeting-action completion. Check privacy, recording consent and retention first.

Read the communications guide → compare related apps → check pilot evidence.

These are planning paths; do not infer telephony, CRM or email integrations are live.
