Identify the kind of resource

Local browser tool

A calculator, text composer or checklist can run in the browser. Reproduce its stated output and check its limits; the presence of AI in a name does not establish a model connection.

Manual guide or prototype

A guide helps a person plan or review work. A prototype may have unfinished features. Check which actions are implemented and which still require a person.

Connected integration

Confirm the actual service connection, data flow, permissions and tested version. A published page or source repository alone does not establish that an integration works.

Five checks for a useful pilot

  1. Define the task

    Name the process owner, the current baseline and one measurable success criterion. Keep the first trial small and reversible.

  2. Inspect the inputs

    Start with fictional or authorized, sanitized examples. Check data handling and dependencies before entering business records; keep credentials and private customer data out of public tools.

  3. Reproduce an outcome

    Compare the output with an expected result. Try empty or invalid input, review error messages and check what happens when an assumption changes.

  4. Review the human decision

    Identify who reviews the output and who may approve, stop or reverse the trial. A draft, scenario or checklist does not authorize sending, purchasing or dispatch.

  5. Record the evidence

    Save the exact URL or version, the example used, the observed result, the limits and the review decision. Record untested features as untested.

A short evidence record

Keep this record in your approved internal workspace. This page is a reading guide and has no submission form.

Resource and version
URL, source reference and date of the test
Example and expectation
Fictional input and the outcome you expected
Observed behavior
Actual output, errors and recovery behavior
Limits and dependencies
What was not tested and what relies on another service
Owner and decision
Reviewer, next small step and stop condition

Worked review: meeting notes

Use fictional notes containing one decision, one owner and one deadline. Compare the draft with those three facts, then repeat with the deadline omitted. A useful result should keep the missing deadline visible instead of inventing one. A person reviews the final text before sharing it.
