Plan a measurable FinOps pilot

Plan one measurable cloud-cost pilot: define its scope, record assumptions and decide what evidence a reviewer needs before expanding it. Use the separate estimator to compare scenarios.

  1. Use a recent invoice as a baseline and record the workloads in scope.
  2. Compare conservative and optimistic assumptions; include the operating and implementation costs.
  3. Use ROI, SLA and governance tools to agree a reversible pilot and compare actual invoices afterward.

Record the decision and its evidence

  1. Name the human owner, workloads, measurement period and stop condition before starting.
  2. Compare actual costs for the same scope and service quality. Review taxes, migration impact and other exclusions separately; the simple model does not include them.
  3. Record whether to continue, revise or stop, together with the evidence and review date. A modeled result does not authorize a purchase or implementation.

Illustrative planning only, not a quote, guaranteed saving, audit or financial advice. All money is USD; no exchange-rate conversion. Inputs and results stay in this page; no account connection, transmission or storage.

Gross monthly reduction = baseline × reduction / 100. Net monthly benefit = gross reduction − monthly operating cost. Horizon benefit = net monthly benefit × months − implementation cost. Simple payback = implementation cost / positive net monthly benefit. Excludes taxes, discounting, growth, migration disruption and workload-specific pricing.

Does this connect to a cloud provider?

No. It calculates only the values you enter, locally in the browser.

Is the reduction percentage a prediction?

No. You choose the percentage; validate it using a measured pilot.

What does a negative result mean?

Under your assumptions, ongoing costs or implementation costs outweigh the modeled reduction.
