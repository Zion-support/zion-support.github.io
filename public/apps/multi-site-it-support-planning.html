Field service planning

Plan IT support across multiple sites

A useful support proposal starts with a consistent record for every location. Use this guide to prepare comparable requirements, clarify what an onsite visit includes and separate confirmed facts from assumptions before requesting country-specific quotations.

Site recordsService windowsWorkloadCommercial modelsCosts and scopeApproval and evidence

Build one record for each site

Give every location a unique reference and record its country, city, address, site type and business owner in your approved working document. Mark each entry as confirmed or awaiting validation, with the date and source. Group locations by practical service area; a provider’s presence in a capital does not establish coverage elsewhere.

  • Describe the work: user support, device installation, printer connection, network checks or a warranty replacement.
  • Record access approval, escort requirements, loading restrictions, parking and required tools.
  • Separate the local contact, remote service desk and person authorized to accept the work.
  • Keep exact addresses, personal contacts and device identifiers in the authorized project channel.

Specify the actual service window

Distinguish opening hours from permitted maintenance hours and requested support coverage. Record the local date, time zone and UTC offset applicable on that date. Write an overnight window explicitly: Monday 22:00 to Tuesday 02:00, not simply “22–02”. Flag weekends, public holidays and any daylight-saving change for confirmation.

Define when the response clock starts, who provides remote triage and whether the target concerns acknowledgement, arrival, restoration or resolution. Confirm regional travel and technician availability before describing an onsite response as committed.

Measure demand before choosing a model

Count named users, peak simultaneous users and devices separately. Break devices down into supported categories and identify shared printers, network equipment and spare units. Summarize available ticket history by period, category, duration and outcome; distinguish incidents, requests, scheduled changes and repeat visits.

Record how many tickets remote support resolves and how many need travel. If history is missing, label the volume as unknown and propose a bounded measurement period. Headcount alone does not establish the number, duration or distribution of onsite visits.

Compare each country separately, with its own currency and conditions. A monthly user rate must not be interpreted as unlimited onsite coverage unless the written scope explicitly provides it.

Make costs and exclusions visible

Request written costs for labor, minimum attendance, travel, parking, accommodation, after-hours work, freight, taxes and applicable import charges. Identify the currency, quote validity, payment terms and cancellation conditions. Separate confirmed amounts from estimates and identify who approves extra work.

State whether parts, consumables, loan equipment, software licenses, manufacturer entitlements, cabling and major installations are included. Agree responsibilities for warranty claims, return shipping and data handling. Avoid adding a benchmark rate or assuming a supplier’s coverage where written evidence is missing.

Approve the scope, then close each visit with evidence

Before any purchase, reservation, supplier commitment or dispatch, obtain formal written client confirmation of the exact scope and price, the corresponding purchase order (PO), and a separate Zion operational release. These are three cumulative requirements.

For each approved visit, agree the ticket reference, work performed, start and finish times, test results, exceptions and customer acceptance. Separate completion from unresolved follow-up work. Review actual volumes and costs before extending the service to more locations.
