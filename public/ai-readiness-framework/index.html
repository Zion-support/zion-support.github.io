Score your AI readiness before you spend on a pilot

Most AI projects fail before the model: on sponsorship, data access, security policy or measurement. A readiness assessment scores six dimensions from 0 to 2, maximum 12, and produces a decision: proceed, prepare or defer. At 10 to 12 you pilot. At 6 to 9 you pilot the strongest use case while fixing the weakest dimension. At 0 to 5 you fix sponsorship, data access and policy first. A zero on data access or security is a blocker regardless of the total.

How the assessment runs

1. Scope and participants

Define the business areas, the participants and the evidence standard.

2. Evidence collection

Gather documents, baselines and system records, not opinions.

3. Scoring with the team

Score together with the people who run the process, 0, 1 or 2 per dimension.

4. Decision

Proceed, prepare, redesign, defer or reject, with the pilot boundary written down.

FAQ

How long does an assessment take?

Typically two to three weeks of evidence collection and workshops, not a self-service questionnaire.

Do we score the whole company?

No. We score per business area, because readiness is rarely uniform across finance, operations and support.

What counts as evidence?

An API document, a recorded baseline, a named owner, an access policy. Opinions are noted separately.

What if we score low?

You fix sponsorship, data access and policy first. A pilot now would measure your gaps, not the AI.

Does a high score guarantee results?

No. It tells you a pilot has the conditions to be measured fairly. It does not guarantee the outcome.
