26 Years of IT, Now AI — The Story Behind Zion Tech Group

---

In 2006, there was no "AI revolution." There was just IT — servers, networks, the unglamorous work of keeping businesses running. That was the starting point. It still is, in a lot of ways.

Zion Tech Group was founded that year in São Paulo, Brazil, by Kleber Garcia Alcatrão. The early years were built on the fundamentals: fixing what was broken, building what was needed, and earning the next engagement by delivering on the last one. No venture capital, no buzzword strategy — just a small team doing IT work that mattered to the people paying for it.

Twenty-six years later, the work has changed. The fundamentals haven't.

From São Paulo to Middletown, Delaware

The move from Brazil to the United States wasn't a rebrand. It was the natural result of a company that had been working across borders for years and needed a home base that matched the client base.

Today, Zion operates from Middletown, Delaware. The team is 33 people. We work with clients in 19 countries. Annual revenue crossed $5.3 million — not by chasing growth for its own sake, but by doing enough good work for enough long-term clients that the growth followed.

Kleber still runs the company. He's still the person who gets on the call for Discovery. That's not a marketing choice — it's just how the company is built. The founder is still in the room when a new client walks in.

The YouTube Archive Nobody Talks About

Before any of this was trendy, we were publishing.

Between 2009 and 2019, Zion produced 11 videos on the company's YouTube channel. They covered IT operations, automation thinking, and the early signs of where the technology was heading. Total views: 5,888.

That number looks small next to the influencer economy. It's not meant to look big. It's meant to show trajectory. Long before "AI agent" was a searchable term, Zion was talking about machines doing work that used to require humans — and publishing that thinking in public, on a schedule, for a decade.

The channel isn't active anymore. The content is still there. The habit of saying what we think, in public, before it's fashionable, hasn't changed.

What Changed: IT Becomes AI

For most of our history, the work was infrastructure. Servers, networks, systems that had to stay up. That work still exists — a lot of our clients still need it — but the center of gravity has moved.

The shift happened gradually, then all at once. First we were automating individual tasks inside IT operations. Then we were connecting tools so data flowed without manual handoffs. Then the tools got smarter — not magic, just better — and the scope of what could be automated expanded. What used to require a custom integration and a dedicated maintenance burden started looking like something you could assemble from composable parts.

That's where Composio entered the picture. We built 31 toolkits on the Composio platform — pre-built connectors and automation patterns that let us stand up AI-powered workflows without reinventing the integration layer every time. Each toolkit represents a repeatable pattern we've used and refined across multiple clients. They're not theoretical. They're what we use.

The point of the toolkits isn't that they exist. It's that we can show up, pick a process, and have a credible starting point within days instead of weeks. That's the same value proposition IT services has always promised — just applied to a different layer of the stack.

The Stack We Actually Run On

Transparency matters. Here's what our own operation looks like:

  • **GitHub Pages** hosts the public site. Static, fast, no unnecessary moving parts.
  • **Stripe** handles payments. Discovery, Consulting, Starter, and Growth tiers all have live checkout links. No "contact us for pricing" — the prices are published.
  • **Calendly** runs scheduling for Discovery calls. One event type, live, no back-and-forth.
  • **WhatsApp** and a **Telegram bot** handle conversation at the channel level — because a lot of our clients, especially in LATAM, prefer to talk where they already work.
  • **MCP marketplace** at [zionmcp.com](https://zionmcp.com) — Model Context Protocol tools and connectors that extend what AI agents can reach into. This is where the Composio toolkits live alongside newer patterns we're building as the ecosystem evolves.

    • None of this is exotic. That's the point. We're not selling a platform. We're selling a team that knows how to connect the tools you already have, add the ones that make sense, and make the whole thing produce measurable results.

    You've seen hype cycles come and go. AI is not the first wave that promised to transform everything. We were around for the automation wave, the cloud wave, the IoT wave. Some of it delivered. Some of it didn't. The skill is knowing which parts of the current wave are real for a specific client before they spend real money on it.

    You've earned the right to say no. Not every problem needs AI. Not every client is a fit. After 26 years, you get to be selective — and that selectivity is itself a selling point, because it means the engagements you do take on are ones you actually believe in.

It's the reason we can take on work that needs coverage across time zones, languages, and domains. The people are real, the coverage is real, and the continuity is real — because the company has been profitable enough to keep them.
