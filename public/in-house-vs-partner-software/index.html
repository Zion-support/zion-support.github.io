In-house, freelancer or partner? Choose by control and speed, not by hourly rate

There is no universally better model. Build in-house when the software is the business and you will iterate on it for years. Hire a partner when you need to start before a hiring cycle could finish. Use a freelancer for a contained, well-defined job. Buy off the shelf when the capability is common and your process can adapt to the tool.

Decision rules

Choose in-house if…

the capability is a differentiator and you have a multi-year roadmap and budget to sustain a team.

Choose a partner if…

you need working software in under six months, or a skill you cannot hire locally.

Choose a freelancer if…

the job is bounded, short and well specified, and you can review it yourself.

Choose a tool if…

the capability is common and your process can adapt to the product.

Choose hybrid if…

you have a core team and need capacity gaps or specialised work at a defined scope.

Refuse to decide by rate if…

you are comparing hourly numbers without time-to-start, handover and continuity.

How the decision is made

1. Discovery

A free online discovery maps the problem, the internal capacity and the time constraint.

2. Scope and model

A bounded scope and the commercial model that matches it: build, partner or buy.

3. Pilot

A measurable pilot with acceptance criteria, so the model is tested before it is scaled.

4. Scale and handover

Documentation, repository handover and a support cadence agreed from the start.

FAQ

How do I compare prices fairly?

Compare total loaded cost, time-to-start and handover, not the hourly rate. Ask for a fixed scope after Discovery.

Who owns the intellectual property?

Contractually. Require full IP, source code in your own repository and documentation.

What if the partner underperforms?

Start with a bounded pilot, require documentation from day one, and agree handover terms in advance.

Do I need an internal product owner?

Yes, for partner-led work. Budget four to eight hours per week for review and decisions.

Can we change model later?

Yes. Many teams start partner-led and move capacity in-house, or the reverse, once the product stabilises.
