Service scoping and planning guide

Build an evidence-based automation vendor risk workflow

Before an automation vendor receives data or production access, define what the workflow will do, who can approve it and which evidence is still missing. Use this guide to prepare a scoped enquiry and a reversible pilot plan.

This is an informational planning resource. It does not scan a vendor, verify certifications, connect to accounts, make a compliance determination or approve a supplier. Implementation scope and feasibility require a separate review.

Start with one bounded use case

Describe the business process, intended users and proposed automated actions. Separate a read-only summary from actions that change records, send messages or move money. Record each system involved, the data categories it holds and the maximum access needed. Name a business owner and a technical reviewer before choosing integrations.

For example, a pilot that drafts a supplier onboarding summary can use synthetic documents and human review. Automatic supplier approval or live account changes should remain outside that pilot unless they receive their own explicit assessment and authorization.

Request evidence without treating claims as proof

  • Data handling: ask which data is collected, where it is processed, how long it is retained and how deletion is verified.
  • Access: document authentication, requested scopes, service accounts, administrative permissions and revocation steps.
  • Assurance: ask for relevant documentation and its date, scope and issuer. Record anything the reviewer cannot independently verify as unknown.
  • Dependencies: identify subprocessors, model providers and external endpoints that the proposed workflow uses.
  • Operations: clarify incident contacts, change notifications, export options and the process for ending access.

A missing document is a review item, not automatic proof that a vendor is unsafe. Conversely, a marketing statement or a numerical score does not establish that a control exists.

Keep a review record a human can audit

For each question, capture the vendor response, evidence reference, observation date, responsible reviewer, unresolved gap and next action. Keep factual observations separate from judgments. Avoid including credentials, customer records or confidential contracts in public tools or an initial enquiry.

Use three explicit outcomes: ready for a limited pilot, needs more evidence, or outside the approved scope. Your organization sets its own acceptance criteria. Legal, privacy and security decisions should go to the responsible qualified reviewers.

Define the pilot gate and stop conditions

  1. Choose synthetic or approved non-sensitive inputs and a sandbox with minimal permissions.
  2. Measure a baseline such as reviewer time per completed assessment; define how errors and missing evidence will be counted.
  3. Require human approval before external messages or changes to authoritative systems.
  4. Specify a stop condition, an owner who can revoke access and a rollback procedure tested before production consideration.
  5. Review the results and remaining unknowns before making a separate deployment decision.

Possible deliverables to discuss include an evidence checklist, access inventory, review-state workflow and pilot acceptance plan. These are proposed scoping items, not a commitment to a particular integration, delivery date or outcome.

Prepare an email-first enquiry

Include a short non-sensitive process description, systems involved, expected action types, your decision owner and the questions blocking your assessment. Do not send passwords, API keys, private vendor documents or personal data.
